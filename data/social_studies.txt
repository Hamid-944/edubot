SOCIAL STUDIES – School Study Material

=== CONTINENTS AND OCEANS ===
Earth's land is divided into seven continents: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, and Australia (also called Oceania). Asia is the largest continent in both size and population. It covers about 30% of Earth's total land area and is home to over 4 billion people. Countries in Asia include China, India, Japan, Russia, and Saudi Arabia.

Africa is the second-largest continent and is sometimes called the "cradle of humanity" because the earliest humans evolved there. It has 54 recognized countries, more than any other continent. The Sahara Desert in Africa is the largest hot desert in the world. The Nile River, which flows through northeastern Africa, is the longest river in the world.

Europe is a relatively small continent but has had a huge influence on world history, culture, and exploration. It contains about 50 countries, including France, Germany, United Kingdom, Italy, and Spain. North America includes the United States, Canada, Mexico, and the countries of Central America and the Caribbean. South America is known for the Amazon Rainforest, which is the largest tropical rainforest in the world. The Amazon River carries more water than any other river on Earth.

Antarctica is the coldest, driest, and windiest continent and is covered by a massive ice sheet. No permanent human population lives there, but scientists operate research stations. Australia is both a continent and a country. It is known for its unique wildlife, including kangaroos, koalas, and the platypus.

Earth has five oceans: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern (Antarctic), and Arctic. The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean. The Atlantic Ocean separates North and South America from Europe and Africa. The Indian Ocean is the warmest ocean.

=== MAPS AND GEOGRAPHY ===
A map is a flat representation of Earth's surface or part of it. Maps use symbols, colors, and keys (legends) to represent real-world features. A compass rose on a map shows the four cardinal directions: North, South, East, and West.

Lines of latitude (parallels) run horizontally around the globe and measure distance north or south of the equator. The equator is the imaginary line at 0° latitude that divides Earth into the Northern and Southern hemispheres. Lines of longitude (meridians) run vertically and measure distance east or west of the Prime Meridian (0° longitude), which runs through Greenwich, England.

Physical maps show natural features like mountains, rivers, and deserts. Political maps show borders between countries and the locations of cities. Climate maps show weather patterns and temperature zones. Population density maps show where people live.

A hemisphere is half of the globe. The Northern Hemisphere contains most of Earth's land and population. The Southern Hemisphere contains most of Earth's oceans. The Eastern Hemisphere includes Europe, Asia, Africa, and Australia. The Western Hemisphere includes the Americas.

=== TYPES OF GOVERNMENT ===
A government is the system by which a country or community is ruled. There are several main types of government.

A democracy is a system in which the people hold power. In a direct democracy, citizens vote on laws and decisions directly. In a representative democracy (also called a republic), citizens elect representatives to make decisions on their behalf. The United States, India, and most European nations are representative democracies.

A monarchy is a system where a single ruler (a king or queen) holds power. In an absolute monarchy, the ruler has total power. In a constitutional monarchy, the ruler's power is limited by a constitution and the people are governed by elected representatives. The United Kingdom and Japan are constitutional monarchies.

In a dictatorship or authoritarian government, one person or a small group holds all power and citizens have few rights. A theocracy is a government ruled by religious leaders or based on religious law.

Democracy features include free elections, the rule of law, protection of individual rights, freedom of speech, and freedom of the press. Citizens in a democracy have the right and responsibility to vote, obey the law, pay taxes, and participate in civic life.

=== ANCIENT CIVILIZATIONS ===
Ancient Egypt was one of the world's first great civilizations, developing along the Nile River around 3100 BCE. The Egyptians are famous for building the pyramids, which served as tombs for pharaohs (Egyptian rulers). They invented hieroglyphics, one of the earliest writing systems. Egyptian society was highly organized, with pharaohs at the top, then priests and nobles, then craftspeople and farmers, and at the bottom, slaves and servants.

Ancient Greece developed around 800 BCE and made lasting contributions to democracy, philosophy, science, art, and literature. Athens was one of the most powerful Greek city-states and is often called the birthplace of democracy. Famous Greek thinkers include Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle (philosophy), Pythagoras and Euclid (mathematics), Hippocrates (medicine), and Homer (epic poetry — The Iliad and The Odyssey).

The Roman Empire was one of the largest empires in history, at its peak controlling much of Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East. Rome began as a republic around 509 BCE, with elected senators making laws. Julius Caesar became a powerful leader and was assassinated in 44 BCE. Augustus Caesar became the first Roman Emperor in 27 BCE. The Romans built roads, aqueducts, and grand buildings like the Colosseum. Latin, the language of Rome, became the basis for French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, and Romanian.

The ancient civilizations of Mesopotamia (between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in modern-day Iraq) were among the first to develop writing (cuneiform), cities, and legal systems. The Code of Hammurabi was one of the earliest written sets of laws, created by the Babylonian king Hammurabi around 1754 BCE.

=== HUMAN RIGHTS AND DEMOCRACY ===
Human rights are the basic rights and freedoms that every person is entitled to, regardless of race, nationality, gender, religion, or any other status. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) was adopted by the United Nations in 1948. It includes rights such as the right to life, liberty, and security; the right to education; the right to work; freedom of thought, conscience, and religion; and freedom from torture and slavery.

The United Nations (UN) is an international organization founded in 1945 with the goal of promoting peace, cooperation, and human rights among nations. It has 193 member states. Its main bodies include the General Assembly (where all nations are represented), the Security Council (responsible for international peace and security), and the International Court of Justice.

Civil rights are the rights of citizens to political and social freedom and equality. The Civil Rights Movement in the United States (1950s–1960s) fought against racial segregation and discrimination. Key figures include Rosa Parks (who refused to give up her seat on a segregated bus) and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. (who led peaceful protests and delivered the famous "I Have a Dream" speech). The Civil Rights Act of 1964 outlawed discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.

=== ECONOMICS: BASIC CONCEPTS ===
Economics is the study of how people and societies use limited resources to meet unlimited wants. Scarcity means there are not enough resources to satisfy all human wants, so people must make choices.

Needs are things necessary for survival: food, water, shelter, and clothing. Wants are things that are desired but not necessary for survival: smartphones, video games, vacations. Trade-offs occur when choosing one option means giving up another.

There are three main economic systems. In a market economy (capitalism), decisions about production and prices are made by individuals and businesses based on supply and demand. In a command economy, the government controls what is produced, how it is produced, and how it is distributed. In a mixed economy, both the government and private businesses play important roles; most countries today have mixed economies.

Supply is the amount of a product available for sale. Demand is how much consumers want to buy a product. When demand increases and supply stays the same, prices usually rise. When supply increases and demand stays the same, prices usually fall.

Taxes are money collected by the government from citizens and businesses to fund public services like schools, roads, hospitals, and the military. Trade between countries allows nations to specialize in producing goods they are efficient at making and exchanging them for goods produced more efficiently elsewhere. Imports are goods brought into a country. Exports are goods sent out of a country.
